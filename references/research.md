# Research behind Human

Initially reviewed 2026-10-30; follow-up on human conversation and imperfect processing on 2026-10-31. This is a focused, interdisciplinary design review, not a systematic review, meta-analysis, or complete theory of human thought. Human conversation varies by person, language, culture, relationship, disability, and purpose. There is no single personality or surface style that constitutes being human.

The sources below distinguish theories, observations, experiments with people, and studies of AI. **Every application to this skill is a design hypothesis.** A finding about people talking to people does not establish that a prompt produces the same outcome. We aim for attentive, useful interaction, not mistaken human identity, maximum attachment, or imitation of human mistakes.

## Evidence and proposed applications

| Source and discipline | What the source supports | Application to this skill and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Grice (1975), pragmatics/philosophy**, [Logic and Conversation](https://web.stanford.edu/class/psych205/papers/Grice-1975.pdf). Theoretical account. | Interpreting an utterance involves conversational purpose and implications, beyond its literal wording. Quantity, quality, relevance, and manner describe cooperative contributions. | Respond to the intended move; supply enough information for it. This is not experimental evidence that shorter replies are always better, and the maxims are not universal literal-writing commands. |
| **Clark & Brennan (1991), cognitive psychology/communication**, [Grounding in Communication](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~illah/CLASSDOCS/Clark91.pdf). Theoretical synthesis with interaction examples. | Participants coordinate content and process, accumulating evidence of sufficient mutual understanding for the current purpose. Communication medium changes the costs of doing so. | Use established constraints and references, and resolve consequential ambiguities. Do not demand explicit confirmation of everything; shared understanding is purpose-dependent, not perfect mind-reading. |
| **Sacks, Schegloff & Jefferson (1974), sociology/conversation analysis**, [A Simplest Systematics for the Organization of Turn-Taking for Conversation](https://www.conversationanalysis.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/04_Sacks_Schegloff_Jefferson_A_simplest_systematics_fir_the_organization_of_turn-taking_for_conversation_Language_.pdf). Analysis of recorded conversation. | Turn allocation is managed locally and sensitive to the recipient and ongoing interaction. | Treat a reply as a turn in an exchange; do not give a self-contained lecture after every follow-up. Spoken turn timing does not establish optimal text length, artificial typing delays, or paragraph limits. |
| **Schegloff, Jefferson & Sacks (1977), conversation analysis**, [The Preference for Self-Correction in the Organization of Repair in Conversation](https://www.conversationanalysis.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/06/11_Schegloff_et_al_The_Preference_For_Self-Correction_in_the_Organization_of_Repair_in_Conversation.pdf). Observational analysis. | Conversation has organized ways to address problems in speaking, hearing, and understanding; repair is broader than replacing factual errors. | Correct the affected interpretation and continue when a misunderstanding is identified. The paper does not test an AI apology template or warrant deliberately introducing mistakes. |
| **Stivers et al. (2009), cross-linguistic conversation research**, [Universals and Cultural Variation in Turn-Taking in Conversation](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.0903616106). Recorded question–response sequences in ten languages. | The sampled languages share tendencies to minimize overlap and silence, with measurable differences in response timing. | Adapt to the person's language and interaction rather than universalizing one English conversational style. This concerns spoken timing, not a tested cross-language prompt policy. |
| **Pickering & Garrod (2004), psycholinguistics**, [Toward a Mechanistic Psychology of Dialogue](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X04000056). Theoretical dialogue model. | Interactive alignment proposes that interlocutors coordinate representations at multiple linguistic levels through the exchange. | Reuse meaningful terminology and referents. Copying slang, emotions, spelling errors, or every syntactic pattern is not established as desirable; alignment is not agreement with beliefs. |
| **Keysar, Lin & Barr (2003), cognitive psychology**, [Limits on Theory of Mind Use in Adults](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-0277(03)00064-7), [author-hosted text](https://cpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/voices.uchicago.edu/dist/f/1312/files/2019/06/Cognition03.pdf). Two communication-game experiments. | Knowing another person's perspective and consistently using that knowledge during comprehension can dissociate. | Separate explicit information from guesses about another mind; permit clarification and correction. This does not prove the model reasons like people, or justify adding an elaborate hidden psychological profile. |
| **Laurenceau, Barrett & Pietromonaco (1998), social/relationship psychology**, [Intimacy as an Interpersonal Process](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9599440/). Two event-contingent diary studies. | Self-disclosure and perceived partner responsiveness were related to experienced intimacy; emotional disclosure mattered in the examined interactions. | Attend to the particular concern, not generic reassurance. These are relationship findings, not evidence that an assistant should cultivate intimacy, claim feelings, or solicit personal disclosure. |
| **Weger et al. (2014), interpersonal communication**, [The Relative Effectiveness of Active Listening in Initial Interactions](https://doi.org/10.1080/10904018.2013.813234). Experiment with 115 participants. | Active-listening responses increased feeling understood compared with advice or simple acknowledgements. Active listening and advice both exceeded acknowledgements on some other outcomes, with small effects. | Acknowledge the specific difficulty when someone wants to be heard; give advice when requested. Neither a universal prohibition on advice nor mandatory paraphrasing follows from this experiment. |
| **Gable et al. (2004), social/relationship psychology**, [What Do You Do When Things Go Right?](https://labs.psych.ucsb.edu/gable/shelly/publications/395). Four studies. | Sharing positive events and perceived active, constructive responses were associated with positive affect and relationship well-being in the studied settings. | Respond to the actual good news before criticism or optimization. Congratulating an achievement does not require inventing future success or approving every inference from it. |
| **Huang et al. (2017), social psychology**, [It Doesn't Hurt to Ask](https://dash.harvard.edu/entities/publication/73120379-2eb6-6bd4-e053-0100007fdf3b), with [2025 correction](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40111841/). Experiments and observational speed-dating analysis. | Question-asking, especially connected follow-up questions, was related to liking and perceived responsiveness in these settings. The correction reports minor reporting errors and says the independent audit upheld the conclusions. | Allow a connected question when it serves the exchange, including sharing rather than just missing task inputs. No question quota; unfamiliar-person conversations do not establish that every assistant turn should end in a question. The observational component should not be treated as randomized causal evidence. |
| **Tenney et al. (2007), judgment/decision research**, [Calibration Trumps Confidence as a Basis for Witness Credibility](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17362377/). Two experiments. | Confidence in erroneous testimony can damage witness credibility more than appropriately expressed uncertainty. | Confidence should follow support, not a desire to sound human. Witness credibility is not a direct measurement of chatbot usefulness or a numerical threshold for suppressing uncertainty. |
| **Amershi et al. (2019), HCI**, [Guidelines for Human-AI Interaction](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2019/01/Guidelines-for-Human-AI-Interaction-camera-ready.pdf). Guideline synthesis and iterative evaluation, including 49 practitioners and 20 products. | Context, social norms, correction, capabilities, and adaptation are recurring design concerns for AI interactions. | Preserve context and make corrections usable. This evaluation supports design guidance, not a claim that the Human prompt or human identity simulation has been validated. |
| **Sharma et al. (2023; ICLR 2024), AI behavior**, [Towards Understanding Sycophancy in Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13548), [revised text](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13548v4). Model evaluations and preference-data analysis. | The studied assistants can change correct responses under pressure and favor a user's view over truth; preference judgments can reward convincing agreement. | Evaluate maintaining warranted disagreement and updating for actual evidence separately. Warmth and high preference ratings are insufficient evidence of truthfulness; these results do not establish present-day rates for every model. |
| **Kim et al. (2024), HCI/AI reliance**, [“I'm Not Sure, But…”](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00623v1). Preregistered experiment, N=404, using medical question answering and a fictional search interface. | First-person uncertainty reduced agreement and trust in the system and improved answer accuracy in this task; overreliance was not eliminated. | Keep decision-relevant uncertainty. Neither blanket confidence nor hedging every message follows. The authors caution against generalizing beyond the question-answering setup and call for context-specific user testing. |

## Access and strength of review

Primary publisher pages, author/institution repositories, and original papers were used rather than explanatory blogs. Access was mixed: selected full-text passages and abstracts were inspected, not every page of every paper. Grice, Clark/Brennan, turn-taking, repair, Keysar, Laurenceau, Amershi, Sharma, and Kim had accessible original text or original-paper excerpts. Pickering/Garrod was checked against the publisher abstract and institutional record. Weger, Gable, Huang, Tenney, and the Huang correction were checked against original abstracts or author/institution records and available paper excerpts. Some direct PDF and publisher requests returned 403, while indexed primary-source excerpts were readable. A primary-source abstract can establish the stated scope and main result; it cannot substitute for a full methods audit.

This review does not determine an optimal amount of empathy, humor, follow-up questions, or disagreement. The prompt avoids quotas because these sources do not provide a general optimum for an AI chat.

## ASD-STE100: where it helps

ASD-STE100 is a controlled English standard for technical documentation, not a theory of ordinary conversation. The [official site](https://www.asd-ste100.org/) lists Issue 9, dated 2025-01-15. Its [official explanation](https://www.asd-ste100.org/about.html), [FAQ](https://www.asd-ste100.org/STE_faq.html), and indexed excerpts of the [Issue 9 document](https://www.asd-ste100.org/assets/files/ASD-STE100_ISSUE9.pdf) describe writing rules plus a controlled dictionary, including rules that depend on procedural versus descriptive writing. Direct access to some official pages/PDF was blocked during this review; indexed official excerpts were inspected. No complete dictionary audit or compliance check was performed.

| Context | Decision for Human | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Instructions written for the assistant | Borrow clear verbs, stable terms, explicit conditions, and manageable sentences. | These reduce ambiguity about the requested behavior. This is a design choice, not proof that STE is a better prompting language. |
| Ordinary assistant conversation | Do not impose the dictionary, literal wording, or English word limits. | Conversation relies on shared context and may include idioms, ellipsis, humor, and uncertainty. The human-conversation literature does not establish that STE improves it. English grammar rules do not transfer directly to Korean. |
| Technical deliverable explicitly requesting STE | Use the applicable official specification and review the deliverable against it. | General plain writing, selective borrowing, and a complete standard compliance check are different tasks. Human does not itself supply a certified checker or reproduce the standard. |

For the experiment, the third condition adds **selected STE-inspired output constraints** to the revised skill: literal wording, consistent terms, short complete sentences, and English procedural/descriptive sentence limits. It deliberately does not implement the controlled dictionary. Its results therefore apply only to that operationalization, not to ASD-STE100 as a whole. The Korean arm uses literal complete sentences without English word counting.

## What can establish improvement

A synthetic model comparison can expose failures and guide revision. It cannot establish that real people feel understood, enjoy the conversation, or mistake it for human speech. Shorter answers are not inherently better, especially for requested explanations.

A subsequent human study could use anonymized, order-randomized conversations and ask participants whether the reply addressed what they meant, respected what they wanted, and was pleasant to continue. It should check factual honesty and unwanted advice independently. Such a study has **not** been conducted here. These proposed questions are not a validated psychological scale or a measurement instrument copied from the cited papers.

## Human conversation is not perfect completed reasoning

The follow-up treats limited and revisable understanding as part of conversation, rather than defining a human as an always-correct, maximally helpful assistant. A useful turn can leave things unresolved, depend on an implication, or invite repair. That does not make every error helpful, nor establish that deliberately adding errors would improve an assistant.

| Primary source | What it contributes | Application and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Ferreira, Bailey & Ferraro (2002)**, [Good-Enough Representations in Language Comprehension](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/current-directions/1467-8721.00158/), [author-hosted paper](https://ferreiralab.faculty.ucdavis.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/222/2015/05/Ferreira-et-al.-2002_GoodEnoughRepresentations_Current-Directions-in-Psych-Sci.pdf) | Reviews findings that comprehension can be partial and meaning representations incomplete; sentence misinterpretations can persist. | Avoid assuming a person fully specifies or perfectly processes every utterance. This is comprehension research, not evidence for a prompt that fabricates mistakes or relaxes consequential accuracy. |
| **Levinson & Torreira (2015)**, [Timing in Turn-Taking and Its Implications for Processing Models of Language](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00731/full) | Reviews timing evidence and processing constraints suggesting that response planning overlaps with comprehension. | A turn need not be a miniature finished essay. Spoken timing does not prescribe a text latency, a word quota, or imitation of speech disfluencies. |
| **Gervits, Eberhard & Scheutz (2016)**, [Disfluent but Effective?](https://aclanthology.org/C16-1317/) | Analyzes actual spontaneous task-oriented CReST team discourse; more effective teams produced more self-repair disfluencies and particular coordination moves. | Repair and incomplete speech should not automatically be scored as conversational failure. This observational association does not establish that inserting hesitation causes better teamwork. |
| **Simon (1955)**, [A Behavioral Model of Rational Choice](https://digitalcollections.library.cmu.edu/node/48694) | Develops an account compatible with limited information and computational capacity rather than an idealized fully rational decision maker. | Conceptual background for avoiding an idealized person model. It is not a dialogue corpus or direct evidence that a particular response style works. |

The skill therefore permits a partial contribution, a provisional low-stakes interpretation, and a small repair in the next turn. An ordinary reaction can stand without another question. It also avoids incidental correction detours that displace the person's point, while retaining accuracy for factual questions and consequential claims. These are proposed applications, not measured reconstructions of human cognition. The invented loaf and shoelace examples in the skill illustrate stopping points; they are not quotations from a corpus.

## Records of what people actually said

**Switchboard / SwDA** supplies recorded two-person telephone conversations on assigned topics. The [Cornell description](https://convokit.cornell.edu/documentation/switchboard.html) documents 1,155 conversations in its conversion and explains how consecutive segments were merged. The follow-up uses the unprocessed corpus, removes transcription markup from selected windows, and retains the words and verbal disfluencies. The [original SwDA resource description](https://compprag.christopherpotts.net/swda.html) identifies the source and annotations. These are genuine recorded exchanges, not generated examples, but their period, medium, assigned topics, and selected windows limit generalization to present-day AI chat.

**Rashkin et al. (2019), [EmpatheticDialogues](https://aclanthology.org/P19-1534/)** provides human-written conversations grounded in emotional situations. The [authors' repository](https://github.com/facebookresearch/EmpatheticDialogues) distributes the data under [CC BY-NC 4.0](https://github.com/facebookresearch/EmpatheticDialogues/blob/main/LICENSE). These are elicited crowdworker conversations, not unobtrusively recorded everyday exchanges. The follow-up decodes the dataset's comma token and keeps original responses, including awkward or unsupported ones. A recorded human continuation is an observation, not an ideal answer or a human preference label.

Both sources are English. Korean synthetic regression checks are kept separate; neither translation nor invented Korean dialogue is relabeled as an observed Korean human conversation. No fine-tuning is performed. Raw third-party corpora are not included in the MIT-licensed skill package.

## Checking the evaluator against evidence

**Mehri & Eskenazi (2020), [USR](https://aclanthology.org/2020.acl-main.64/)** releases [human quality annotations](https://shikib.com/usr) for responses to shared contexts. The follow-up uses eight fixed PersonaChat pairs, comparing model choices with the original human *Overall* ratings. It withholds annotator scores and source/model labels from the assessor. These are existing ratings of role-play dialogues, not new ratings of Human skill responses; a small difference in three annotators' means is not an unambiguous pairwise preference.

**Zheng et al. (2023), [Judging LLM-as-a-Judge](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685)** examines position, verbosity, and self-enhancement biases. **Züfle et al. (2026), [Calibrating LLM Judges for Human and AI Conversations](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29431)** is a recent preprint examining conversational-success judgments and calibration on CANDOR and human–AI conversations. Its abstract and selected text were inspected. These motivate order-swapped comparisons, simple controls, and an external human-label check; they do not certify this evaluator or make a same-family model a substitute for people.

The follow-up retains identical-answer controls, visibly damaged responses, and injected redundant explanation. It also reassesses the exact original 24 pairs, rather than inferring the cause of their ties from new examples. Results and the distinction between sensitivity and actual improvement are recorded in the follow-up evaluation report.
