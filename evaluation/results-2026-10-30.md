# Conversation comparison — 2026-10-30

The revision incorporates research-informed behaviors, but **this run did not establish that it produces more human-like conversation than the original**. The anonymous model assessor tied the original and revision in all 24 paired dialogue comparisons. This is a negative/inconclusive result for the claimed behavioral improvement, not a human validation result.

## Method

- Baseline: commit `689f6c9`; revised skill SHA-256 `f7c6467b6bedb810cecaf89536cc5cbcc4d33d10ccc4a55a83e041dfb8bcbd68`.
- Generation and assessment: `gpt-6.1-sol`, medium reasoning, Codex CLI `0.166.0-alpha.5.1`, 2026-10-30 KST.
- Main set: 10 scenarios × 2 repetitions × 3 conditions = 60 dialogues and 84 assistant replies.
- Supplementary set: 4 scenarios × 1 repetition × 3 conditions = 12 dialogues and 24 assistant replies. These cases were frozen after inspecting some main-set responses, before their own generation and aggregate main-set scores. No skill edit occurred between sets. They are not independent human validation.
- Total: 14 scenarios, 72 dialogues, **108 assistant replies**; each condition has 24 dialogues and 36 replies. Korean and English are equally represented by scenario count. Some dialogues contain two real generated assistant turns.
- Every turn was generated in a fresh ephemeral session, receiving only preceding messages and that condition's own earlier replies. Future user turns and expected checks were withheld.
- Skill bodies were supplied in otherwise matched prompts. User config was ignored, project-document loading was disabled, and web search was disabled. The common Codex base environment remained shared, including installed discovery metadata. This tests the supplied skill instructions, not automatic skill selection or an empty base model.
- Zero observed tool events across 108 generated replies and 24 assessment calls. Inputs, outputs, raw events, settings, and snapshot hashes were retained locally.
- The assessor saw randomly shuffled anonymous labels, the actual transcripts, and the frozen local rubric. It did not receive skill text or condition names. It assessed all three versions together once per case/repetition; this is not three independent human ratings.

The main-set original/revised comparisons were 20 ties out of 20. The supplementary comparisons were 4 ties out of 4. Each language therefore has 12 ties out of 12 for original versus revised.

## What differed

In the second Korean joke repetition, the original and revision extended the user's farming joke, while the STE-inspired reply mostly sympathized about increasing bugs. In the supplementary family-business exchange, the original and revision initially asked about the user's own experience, while the STE-inspired version asked for more details about the father's reaction. The assessor preferred each unconstrained version to the constrained version in those two dialogues.

The revision received one additional rubric point in a supplementary travel exchange by retaining uncertainty about next week's weather. The original inferred that having no reservation removed the burden of rescheduling, which was slightly broader than the stated evidence. The assessor nevertheless treated the pair as tied. A tiny mean-score difference must not override the all-tie pairwise result.

The wording review also found repeated reflective paraphrases in both original and revised replies. Adding an instruction against formulaic responses did not eliminate that behavior. The revision is longer (1008 whitespace-separated words including frontmatter versus 758 in the original); its additional prompt cost has not been justified by a demonstrated preference advantage here.

## ASD-STE100 interpretation

The third arm added selected **STE-inspired output instructions**, including literal language, consistent terms, short complete sentences, and English procedural/descriptive sentence limits. It did not implement the approved dictionary or undergo a full standard-compliance audit. The Korean arm did not apply English word counting. Generated adherence to all selected constraints was not independently quantified, so the findings concern adding those instructions, not a verified fully constrained output style.

The two preference differences are insufficient to conclude that ASD-STE100 is generally worse for conversation. They do not test whether STE improves the wording of instructions supplied to an LLM. The adopted design borrows plain wording for instructions, leaves everyday conversation flexible, and reserves a formal STE check for technical deliverables that request it. See [research and limits](../references/research.md).

## Limits and next decision

Cases were constructed by the authoring assistant from the design requirements. One model family generated and assessed the responses. The evaluator may favor its own style, and anonymous labels cannot hide all stylistic clues. Scores are near the ceiling. Two repeated generations of a case do not represent two independent users. There was no no-skill arm, user study, independent human rating, statistical significance test, or proof of a human-like internal thought process.

The concrete result is a research-documented revision and a reusable regression set. **Improved perceived human likeness remains unverified.** Human review of anonymized conversations is the appropriate next evidence source; it has not been performed here. Full synthetic transcripts and the reproducible local runner are provided as local deliverables rather than publishing local session logs.
